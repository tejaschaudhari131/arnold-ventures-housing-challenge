# Policy Scenario Comparison

Small Homes Policy Lab, five-year window 2027–2031. Tejas Chaudhari, Yoon Yik Ng, and Christopher Severns, Carnegie Mellon University. Outcome: **net new homes completed**, counted when finished. A rebuild that replaces one house with four counts +3. Figures are generated by `src/lab/engine.py` from `config/lab/` and `data/sample/lab/`. Reproduce with `python -m src.lab.results`. The app is `/lab`.

The Lab is the challenge entry. It compares four Handbook benchmarks — ADUs, missing middle (2–6 homes), parking, and a 50 percent fee cut — across all 67 Pennsylvania counties, plus a combined package and an approval-reform extra. Transit-oriented density is not a small-homes reform and is not in the Lab. The Stateframe core engine still runs that scenario for all eleven challenge states; its table is at the end so the two models are not mixed.

## Pennsylvania, statewide

Baseline is 111,016 five-year completions: the 2023–2025 Building Permits Survey run-rate, converted with published completion shares. Pittsburgh's October 2026 ADU ordinance adds 78 homes that the permit window cannot contain yet. Every other column is an increment versus that Baseline.

| Scenario | Central | Envelope p10–p90 | Zoning bound | ACS MOE |
| -------- | ------: | ---------------: | -----------: | ------: |
| Baseline (level) | 111,016 | 106,755–116,798 | 111,007–111,022 | ±6 |
| 1. ADUs | +6,048 | 1,048–8,768 | 4,552–7,183 | ±183 |
| 2. Missing middle (2–6) | +12,916 | 4,269–20,314 | 10,661–14,583 | ±276 |
| 4. Parking | +1,202 | 531–1,543 | 1,202–1,202 | ±0 |
| 5. Fees | +6,790 | 3,544–8,684 | 6,785–6,795 | ±4 |
| Package (extra) | +27,387 | 12,590–37,221 | 23,989–29,900 | ±326 |
| Approval (extra) | +6,817 | 4,688–11,620 | 6,817–6,817 | ±0 |

Three uncertainty layers are reported and never added. The envelope is 200 seeded triangular draws over the published assumptions, except the zoning priors and the locked Handbook fee. The zoning bound moves only those priors. The ACS band moves host counts, rents, and values by their 90 percent margins and combines the effects in quadrature. Stock-weighted evidence coverage is 13.1 percent for ADUs (Philadelphia prohibited, Pittsburgh allowed as of 2026-10-29) and 0 percent for 2–6 homes. Unknown zoning is a prior, never zero.

**Ranking of the four Handbook benchmarks: missing middle > fees > ADUs > parking.** The top pair is a close call: missing middle leads fees by 6,126 homes, but the order flips in 19 percent of assumption draws. It would flip if the homeowner uptake hazard fell to 0.31 percent a year (central 0.6), conversion cost rose to $195,000 (central $160,000), or the cap rate rose to 7.1 percent (central 6). Fifty-six of 67 counties have a close top-benchmark pair. Rebuilds of four or six homes almost never pencil; the missing-middle increment is nearly all `mm2` (keep the house, add one).

**The package is not a sum.** Legalizing ADUs and 2–6 homes on the same lots, resolved one option per lot, delivers 12,971 homes. Adding the two separate scenarios would count 18,964 (competition loss 5,993). The fee cut then adds 11,878 and removing parking minimums adds 2,538, for a package of 27,387 against 26,956 if the four benchmarks are added. The fee cut is worth more inside the package because it also improves the newly legal options.

Implied cost elasticity at central values is 2.29, inside the 1–3 literature range.

## Allegheny County walkthrough

Allegheny is the worked example: 436,257 single-family hosts, ACS 2020–2024, with observed WPRDC lot-area bins (2012 base-year assessed areas; Pittsburgh bins for urban hosts). Pittsburgh's ADU ordinance is observed on the city's 93,348 hosts; the rest of the county is unknown. ADU evidence coverage is 21.4 percent.

| Baseline (level) | 13,775 | 13,238–14,495 | — | ±6 |
| ADUs | +275 | 28–507 | 202–332 | ±22 |
| Missing middle | +857 | 183–1,545 | — | ±57 |
| Parking | +273 | 118–329 | — | ±0 |

Fees lead missing middle here (gap 199; 44 percent of draws flip). The county has more pipeline to move and fewer unknown lots that a legalization reform can unlock. ## New Hampshire portability

New Hampshire is the lower-data test: 10 counties, no WPRDC-style lot file, and a statewide ADU statute (RSA 674:72, detached hosts allowed with parking since 2017) plus SB 284's one-space parking cap (effective 2025-09-13). ADU evidence coverage is 92 percent. Missing-middle coverage is still 0 percent.

| Scenario | NH increment |
| -------- | -----------: |
| Baseline (level) | 22,136 |
| ADUs | +81 |
| Missing middle | +2,344 |
| Parking | +30 |
| Fees | +1,095 |
| Package | +3,761 |
| Approval | +1,364 |

The ADU increment is small because those ADUs are already legal: they sit in Baseline, not in the reform. SB 284's parking-cap response (107 homes) is also in Baseline. Missing middle still leads, and the MM-versus-fees pair is a close call (16 percent of draws). The same code, the same assumption file, and the same three uncertainty layers run both states. Switching New Hampshire on in `/lab` is the portability demonstration. Detail: `docs/NH_PORTABILITY.md`.

## What the Lab will not say

It will not name a parcel, a builder, or a sale price. It will not draft a statute. Philadelphia's ADU prohibition and Pittsburgh's 2026 ordinance are observed; everything else is a prior. Rebuilds of four and six homes are legal under missing middle and almost never feasible at Pennsylvania prices. Applying the Handbook's $50,000 per-home fee to ADUs is a judgment call; the core engine uses $6,000 and reports a much larger ADU increment (see below). Deck and video are out of scope for this repository.

## How to reproduce

```
bash scripts/setup.sh
bash scripts/test.sh
PYTHONPATH=. python -m src.lab.results          # recompute; compare to data/sample/lab/results_*.json
bash scripts/run.sh                             # http://127.0.0.1:8080/lab
```

`scripts/get_lab_data.sh` refreshes extracts (Census, optional WPRDC and HUD). If the network fails it keeps `data/sample/lab/`. A `workflow_dispatch` job, `.github/workflows/lab-results.yml`, reruns the committed-result tests. Fingerprints in `results_PA.json` and `results_NH.json` cover the configs, extracts, and Lab source.

The Lab's Pennsylvania ADU increment (+6,048) applies the Handbook's $50,000 per-home fee to ADUs. The core Stateframe engine uses its own $6,000 ADU fee and reports +46,261. That fee gap is why the two numbers differ. Walking the Lab toward the core, one step at a time: use the $6,000 fee (+12,505); then value the ADU the way a homeowner does — 4.5 percent cap rate, no operating share, rent × 0.85 (+31,489); then drop adoption and build lags (+48,192), within about 4 percent of the core. See the Lab at /lab and the core engine at /. Method: `docs/SMALL_HOMES_LAB.md` §9.

Rank reforms by units inside one model. The primary outcome is homes, not the percent lift. The fee cut is the most stable core-engine reform (about 5 to 7 percent of baseline in ten states). Transit-oriented density is local: 10,789 in Virginia, 8 in New Hampshire. Already-legal shares, station counts, and the $50,000 fee stack are assumptions in the core; the Lab locks that fee for the Handbook comparison and applies it to ADUs.

## Limits that apply to both models

Neither model says which statute to pass, which parcel will redevelop, or what existing homes will sell for. There is no equilibrium price feedback beyond an absorption cap in the core and a five-year hazard in the Lab. Open the Why drawer or "Show the math" before a number leaves the room.
